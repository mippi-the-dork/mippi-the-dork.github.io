## Design Intent

Games is the first major professional section because game design work is the primary reason the portfolio exists. The section should establish that the site is not a general creative portfolio with games as one category. Games are the center, while leadership, consulting, tools, writing, and technical work support that practice.

The section balances one large featured project with smaller paths into consulting, Unreal research, and toolmaking. This gives a visitor both a clear focal point and evidence that the design work extends beyond one title.

## Content Hierarchy

The intended order is:

1. Section statement: `Games are the point.`
2. Project Heavy Water as the featured project.
3. Perspective Visualizer embedded inside Heavy Water.
4. Indie Design Consultation summary.
5. Azure Altar R&D teaser.
6. Unreal Tools callout.

Project Heavy Water receives the most visual and spatial weight because it is the best place to demonstrate active game-design thinking rather than only list credits.

## Professional Reading Goal

A visitor should be able to understand, without interacting with any hidden game systems, that the portfolio emphasizes:

- systems and game design,
- direction and design leadership,
- perspective and player experience experimentation,
- indie consultation,
- Unreal Engine R&D,
- workflow tool creation.

The hidden game layer may reward interactions inside the section, but the professional meaning must remain intact without them.

## Relationship to Other Sections

The Games section deliberately cross-links to other areas rather than duplicating their full content.

- The consulting summary links to Consulting.
- Azure Altar links toward Tools.
- The Unreal Tools callout links to Tools.
- Heavy Water owns the Perspective Visualizer but the visualizer has its own canonical documentation category.

This makes Games a high-value entry point into the rest of the portfolio.

## Design Rules

- The featured project should remain the dominant element.
- Supporting cards should expand the story, not compete with Heavy Water.
- Cross-links should describe why the destination matters before asking the visitor to leave the section.
- The Games section must remain useful if Three.js, progression, achievements, or the Dev Console fail.
- Playful interactions should reinforce the user's identity as a game designer rather than obscure project information.
- The section should be scannable before requiring any deep reading.

## Progression Relationship

Games is one of the six major sections tracked for broad engagement and postgame completion.

Visiting Games can contribute to:

- normal Finale broad-engagement requirements,
- the postgame Grand Tour achievement,
- local session telemetry,
- Player Model interpretation,
- Dynamic Optional Objectives involving section exploration.

The section itself is never gated by progression.

## Success Criteria

The section succeeds when a first-time visitor can answer:

- What kind of games does Mippi design?
- What is the currently featured design problem?
- Does the work extend beyond one project?
- Where can I learn more about consulting or tools?
