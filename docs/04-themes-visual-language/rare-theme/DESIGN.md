## Design Intent

Rare is the starting presentation and must feel like a finished portfolio.

It is not a placeholder waiting for Epic.

The visitor may never discover the progression layer, so Rare must stand on its own as a professional visual system.

## Visual Identity

Rare uses:

- Unreal blue,
- bright cyan,
- cool graphite,
- technical white,
- restrained dark panels,
- subtle blue glow.

The palette supports the site's Unreal Engine and systems-design identity without copying Unreal's UI literally.

## Emotional Tone

Rare should feel:

- technical,
- confident,
- clean,
- exploratory,
- restrained.

It is the calmest rarity.

That calm is important because the site becomes more visually energetic as progression advances.

## Background and Panels

The base page uses very dark cool neutrals.

Blue radial light near the top-left creates depth without becoming a strong gradient wash.

Panels use cool dark grays with low-opacity white and blue borders.

## Accent Hierarchy

Primary blue communicates:

- active control,
- navigation emphasis,
- tool identity,
- interactive affordance.

Cyan is a secondary high-energy accent.

White is used for text and technical highlights.

## Pickup Language

Rare pickups remain part of the blue world.

Examples include:

- blue Hearts,
- cyan Stars,
- blue/cyan Rupees,
- cool-blue Keys,
- blue-white Doug Bones.

The goal is cohesion rather than simulation of original game-source colors.

## Achievement Language

The original 10 achievements are Rare achievements.

Their Ledger rows stay blue even if the site later uses Epic or Legendary as the active theme.

This makes Rare a semantic rarity, not merely the current page palette.

## Attention Cues

Rare attention cues generally use:

- cyan border,
- blue/cyan glow,
- brightness pulse.

Exceptions such as Heart claim readiness may use a semantic pink Heart accent while the surrounding pulse still belongs to the Rare world.

## Theme Position

Rare remains selectable after Epic and Legendary unlock.

This is an intentional player preference.

A completionist can finish the site and still choose the starting visual identity.

## Design Rules

- Rare must remain fully readable and visually complete.
- Do not make Rare look intentionally weak or desaturated to exaggerate Epic.
- Use blue as primary and cyan as high-energy support.
- Preserve dark neutral space around high-intensity effects.
- Rarity-owned Rare components remain Rare blue in every active page theme.
- Higher-tier mechanics can still function while Rare is selected.

## Success Criteria

Rare succeeds when a first-time visitor assumes it is the normal complete site, not the lowest level of an obvious game progression.
